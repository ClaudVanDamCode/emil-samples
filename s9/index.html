360 walk-through sample: smooth moves between points, no WebGL

Drag to look around, scroll or pinch to zoom, click a ring on the floor (or a point on the plan) to walk there.

How this sample works

  • Each capture point is a 360 cube (six square images) drawn with CSS 3D transforms. The browser composites it on the GPU without opening a WebGL context, so it runs on the phones and older laptops where WebGL tours stutter or fail.
  • A move is three things at once: the view turns toward the next point, the camera travels forward inside the current cube, and the next cube fades in while its camera arrives from behind. The heading is kept, so it reads as walking through the house, not as a cut.
  • The floor rings are not placed by hand: they are computed from the camera positions, so every point shows the rings of its neighbours in the right spot.
  • The rooms you see here are placeholders drawn by the page itself, only to show the viewer. With your files, every point shows your rendered 360 images.

With your files

  • Your 360 renders (equirectangular) are converted to cube faces, with a small preview tier that loads first and a sharp tier after, so a move never waits on a download.
  • Your camera file gives each render's position and height: that drives the floor rings, the walking direction and the plan.
  • The fbx model gives the floor plan for the mini map and which points can see each other, so rings only appear where you could really walk.
  • Delivered as a static folder (HTML, CSS, JS, images) that you can host anywhere or embed in your Next.js/React site as one component.
